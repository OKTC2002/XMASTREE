Where Did the Christmas Tree Tradition Come From?
Have you ever wondered how a simple evergreen tree became the star of Christmas? The tradition has roots that go back hundreds of years! Long before Christmas was celebrated, people in many parts of the world brought evergreen branches into their homes during winter as a symbol of life, hope, and the promise of spring. Because evergreens stayed green even when everything else looked brown and lifeless, they were a cheerful reminder that winter wouldn't last forever.

The Christmas tree tradition as we know it is generally traced to 16th-century Germany, where people began bringing decorated evergreen trees into their homes for Christmas. Some stories credit Martin Luther with adding candles to a tree after being inspired by the stars shining through the branches, although historians aren't certain that story is true. The custom eventually spread throughout Europe and became especially popular in England during the 1800s, thanks in part to Queen Victoria and Prince Albert, who were pictured celebrating Christmas around a decorated tree.
